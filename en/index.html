Build your own video database on Windows

Key features

Manage videos on your PC

Select files or folders to add videos in formats such as MP4, MOV, MKV, and AVI. FrameCatalog reads details such as duration and resolution while keeping your files in place.

Find videos at a glance

Browse thumbnails to see what is in your library. Hover over a thumbnail for a short preview without opening each video. Choose any point in a video for its thumbnail.

Organize with your own fields

Create text, date, and choice fields for the information you want to keep. Link a performer to multiple videos, then record details that vary from clip to clip in separate fields.

Narrow down your search

Combine AND or OR tag matching with performer, recording date, resolution, and other filters. Save the filters you use often and apply them again later.

What makes FrameCatalog different

Record details for each performer in a video

In a typical video organizer, you can tag a video with “Performer A,” “Performer B,” “Long hair,” and “Short hair.” Those tags alone don’t tell you which performer has long hair.

In FrameCatalog, you can record details for each performer in each video. Set Performer A’s hairstyle to Long hair and Performer B’s to Short hair, then filter for videos where Performer A has long hair. You can do the same for clothing and other details that change from video to video.

Keep profile information, such as a performer’s birth date and rating, separate from their hairstyle or outfit in a particular video. You can update those per-video details for the same performer across several selected videos. When both dates are available, FrameCatalog calculates and displays the performer’s age at recording.

Find performers and providers by any of their names

The same performer or provider may be credited under different names or spellings from one video to the next. Save those alternate names under one performer or provider in FrameCatalog. Then search using whichever name you remember to find their videos without renaming your files.

Choose what to record and how to enter it

Alongside built-in details such as title, duration, and resolution, you can create your own fields for videos, performers, and providers. Choose both the field name and how you enter its information. Use text for filming locations, dates for purchases, a list of choices for genres, and long text for impressions. Pick the format that fits what you want to record and build a video database of your own.

Explore statistics and open the matching videos

See how your entire library or filtered results break down by performer, tag, or custom field, with counts and percentages. For example, group your videos by genre and select a genre in the breakdown to open the matching videos.

Save alternate names for each performer or provider so you can find their videos under any of those names. Search and filter

Search by file name or entered information. Combine tags, ratings, recording date ranges, resolution, duration, and folders. Match all selected tags with AND, or any of them with OR.

Relink files and back up your library

If you move a video while reorganizing your storage, point its library entry to the new location. If you get a higher-quality copy of the same video, replace the file in that entry. Your tags, ratings, custom fields, and notes carry over, so you can keep the work you put into organizing it.

Back up your library entries, custom fields, thumbnails, previews, and display settings together, and restore them when needed. Back up your original video files separately.

BEFORE YOU START

System and video requirements

For Windows 10 / 11 PCs

FrameCatalog supports 64-bit (x64) Windows PCs. The app is available in English, Japanese, and Simplified Chinese.

Library information, thumbnails, and previews are stored on your PC. Larger collections take longer to add and require free disk space for previews and other generated files.

H.265 requires HEVC support in Windows

Windows must support H.265 (HEVC) to generate thumbnails and previews from HEVC videos. Depending on your setup, you may need to install HEVC Video Extensions separately from Microsoft Store. The extension may require a separate purchase.

Video compatibility depends on the internal codec and profile, as well as your PC’s capabilities, not just the file extension. MPEG-1, MPEG-2, and MPEG-4 Visual use bundled video processing components. H.264, H.265 (HEVC), and VC-1/WMV9 use Windows video features. Support for every video format is not guaranteed. Before purchasing the upgrade, use the free version to test importing, thumbnails, and previews with your own videos.

Adding a video does not convert or overwrite the original file. Previews are short and silent. Click Play to open the full video in your usual player.

QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Questions about organizing your local video library

Do I need to move my video files?

No. Register videos from your PC or external drives and manage them in their original locations.

How is this different from organizing folders?

Find videos across folders using ratings, tags, performers and custom fields. Combine AND or OR tag filters with resolution, duration and recording date.

Can I preview videos without opening each file?

Hover over a thumbnail to play a short, silent preview. For full-length playback, the play button opens your usual video player.

Can I try it for free?

Add up to 50 videos for free and test thumbnails and previews with your own files. A one-time purchase removes the library size limit. There is no monthly subscription.

Where can I get FrameCatalog?

Download it from Microsoft Store for a 64-bit (x64) PC running Windows 10 or 11.
